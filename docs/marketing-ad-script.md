# PetalPop 3D — marketing ad script

**Campaign:** Little blooms. Big feelings.  
**Master cut:** 90 seconds, vertical 9:16, 1080 × 1920, 30 fps.  
**Audience:** Someone making a personal digital gift for a birthday, anniversary, congratulations, thank-you, or a loved one far away.  
**Creative idea:** Follow one bouquet from a birthday idea to a gift someone can open. Every feature helps make that gift more personal.

**Production requirement:** Build a composed motion advertisement with an energetic, evolving instrumental soundtrack. Real app interactions sit inside animated scenes with layered backgrounds, floating cards, botanical details, camera choreography, and kinetic typography. Each of the 15 scenes has its own motion treatment. The viewer should feel a playful gift coming together and understand the controls that create it.

The visual style is playful, tactile, and warm, directly matching the website: cream backgrounds, blush and lilac accents, berry-plum text, soft shadows, rounded cards, and the app's actual 3D flowers. Let the soundtrack drive transitions and decorative motion. The bouquet stays recognizable throughout, and the active control remains stable while its action is being taught.

## The story and demo setup

Mia's birthday is today. Alex builds a bouquet in Mia's favorite colors, adds a little companion, writes a personal letter, and frames a shared memory. The finished gift opens with a short celebration.

Use fictional names and a cleared demo photo. Keep the same photo, note, colors, and arrangement through the editor, shared gift, and PNG card.

- Starting point: **Sweetheart** preset; rose quantity starts at 4.
- Flower edit: raise roses to 6, then add tulips. Keep a modest bouquet so individual blooms are easy to identify.
- Fillers: Baby's Breath and Eucalyptus; show the quantity control and **Stepped fillers**.
- Finish: **Double-Layer Cone**, **Classic Bow**, **Sugar blush** palette; give one flower a custom color.
- Arrangement: show **Natural dome → Stepped bouquet**, then adjust all blooms and one recognizable front rose.
- Final objects: **Cuddle Teddy**, **Heart Balloon**, **Sealed Letter**, and **Memory Frame**. Show the puppy, kitten, and other frame choices in the actual Objects catalog.
- Final effects: **Floating Hearts + Confetti Pop**. Use separate captured variants for the brief butterflies/sparkles examples, with no more than two effects in a variant.
- Gift fields: For **Mia**, From **Alex**, Title **Happy birthday, Mia!**, Body **A little bouquet for your big day.**
- Envelope note: **Happy birthday, Mia!\nHere's to more little adventures together.\nLove, Alex**

## Motion and sound direction

**Compose each scene in layers.** Use a cream stage; a slow blush/lilac background layer; drifting botanical details; the actual bouquet or framed app footage; the active control; then readable captions. Move foreground petals slightly faster than background shapes to create parallax. Keep decorative movement subtle behind the controls and more expressive in transitions and hero shots.

**A continuous petal motif.** One editorial petal leads the eye from the birthday reminder into the studio, past chapter changes, and into the final logo. Give it a curved path, gentle rotation, and a soft shadow. A few secondary petals travel at different depths during hero shots. During teaching shots, the main petal rests near the active control.

**Action → result → hold.** Let the viewer see the tap or slider gesture, its effect on the bouquet, and a short hold on the result. Keep the active control and 3D preview together, using the app's mobile arrangement layout as the visual model. Animate the composition into place, then settle it while the viewer learns. Quantity additions retain the native flower grow-in; the flowers sway between actions.

**Camera choreography.** Use macro-to-wide reveals, small three-quarter orbits, foreground-to-background parallax, and eased pushes into the current feature. Keep control close-ups front-facing. Move the editorial camera during the scene entrance or after the result has registered; hold it while the cursor drags a slider. Alternate wide gift compositions with close detail shots so the 90 seconds have changing visual scale.

**Typography with purpose.** Key verbs arrive with short staggered slides or gentle spring movement on a music beat. Use an odometer-style editorial quantity callout, a growing underline for “Height,” and a widening bracket for “Spread.” Keep the actual control values visible. Use one short headline at a time. “All blooms” and “One bloom” get stable, distinct labels. Captions settle while the corresponding action happens.

**Transitions connected to the subject.** A petal passes close to the camera to open the flower chapter; the wrapper's diagonal edge wipes into styling; an editorial ribbon sweeps into the objects scene; the envelope seal match-cuts into a photo detail; the frame edge reveals the gift. Keep these treatments outside the demonstrated app interaction. Preserve the actual interface, tap target, and visible outcome.

### Background music brief

**Sound:** Playful instrumental electropop with a light disco groove: bouncy rounded bass, bright plucked synth or marimba-like keys, warm chords, crisp but gentle claps, small shakers, and sparkling bell details. It should feel cheerful, stylish, and personal, with a memorable melodic identity matching the app's soft, colorful world.

**Tempo:** **120 BPM, 4/4**. At 30 fps, one beat is 15 frames, making flower taps, card entrances, typography, and transitions easy to synchronize. Use the beat for accents; retain natural timing for the app's actual interactions and envelope reveal.

**Melodic identity:** A short original four-note motif, introduced in the opening, varied through the build, softened for the letter, and brought back at the end. Let quantity tap sounds and copied-link chimes share its tonal character. Keep the vocal frequency range clear for narration.

**Arrangement:** The track needs changes in melody, percussion, bass, and density across the ad. Request or select a cleared track with usable stems: drums/percussion, bass, melody, chords, and decorative sounds, plus a full mix. The music specification here is a production brief; the final track will be chosen or composed during video production.

Use paper rustles, soft plucks, airy slider accents, and warm chimes sparingly so the soundtrack remains coherent. Keep any transition sweeps gentle. Check the mix on phone speakers: narration stays clear, bass does not overwhelm, and the melody remains audible.

**The letter is the emotional pause.** Lower the music slightly when the envelope opens. Keep the app's native reveal at normal speed; allow roughly two seconds for its animation and the appearing note sheet. Save & seal returns to the scene. Decorative paper and ink movement belongs to the surrounding composition.

**Confetti is the brief accent.** Its dedicated clip occupies 01:09–01:12. Use the actual effect, one camera move, a crisp pop, and a soft trailing shimmer. The recipient reveal can include the same native burst as it naturally replays. Keep the foreground clear enough to recognize the complete gift.

## Capture and edit checklist

1. Capture real app footage for every demonstrated interaction. Record extra time before and after each action for editing. Keep motion enabled for flower transitions, envelope reveals, and effects.
2. Capture a native portrait viewport with legible controls. For desktop-only close-ups, crop to the real active control and matching preview; keep the cursor/tap target aligned. Place essential captions away from platform overlays, within a central safe area.
3. Record the birthday gift consistently before creating the closing variations. Record utility experiments in separate takes and restore the main design afterward.
4. Record the entire envelope reveal in both editor and gift view, the photo upload/crop result, and the full native Confetti Pop. Music timing follows these captures.
5. Record the copied link opening in a fresh browser view. The current project supports local demonstrations; public cross-device sharing requires a hosted app and share service. Use the actual available capture environment and add a public destination to the final card only after it is verified.
6. In this script, “letter” means the app's **Sealed Letter** object. Copying a link prepares it for the user to send through their preferred channel. The demonstrated workflow does not include automatic email delivery.
7. Use actual PNG output for the card shot. Interactive envelope opening and motion are demonstrated through the gift view.
8. Watch the cut once with sound off: each chapter should still communicate the action, visible result, and reason to use it. Check phone-size text, subtitle contrast, note/photo legibility, and the difference between All blooms and One bloom.
9. End on a stable, readable brand and CTA for the final three seconds. Suggested post caption: **A bouquet in their colors. A photo of your favorite moment. A letter from you. Make a little joy with PetalPop 3D.**
10. Check all 15 scenes against the motion choreography: each has a composed entrance, readable feature moment, and motivated exit. Keep a living background or native product motion during settled shots. Verify that cream, blush, berry, rounded surfaces, and the app's typography carry consistently through footage, captions, graphics, and the end card.
11. Listen to the complete 90-second music edit. Confirm a recognizable hook, changing arrangements, a softer letter/photo passage, the short confetti payoff, and a resolving ending. Match decorative accents to the rhythm while keeping real interactions understandable.
